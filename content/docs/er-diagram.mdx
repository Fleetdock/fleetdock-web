---
title: ER diagram
description: See a database's tables, columns, keys and the foreign keys between them as an entity-relationship diagram.
---

A database's **Diagram** tab draws its entity-relationship diagram (ERD): every table and view
with its columns, and a line for every foreign key between them. Fleetdock reads it from the
database itself, so it is always the current structure, whichever tool created it.

## Reading the diagram

- Each card is a table, or a view with a dashed border. The header shows its name (on
  PostgreSQL with the schema) and the engine's row estimate.
- Columns are listed in order with their type. A key icon marks the primary key, a link icon
  a column used by a foreign key, and `?` a column that accepts `NULL`. Very wide tables list
  their first 24 columns plus every key column, and say how many more there are.
- An arrow runs from the referencing column to the column it references: from
  `orders.customer_id` to `customers.id`. A **dashed** line is an optional relation, where
  every referencing column accepts `NULL`.

Click a table to highlight its relations and the columns they join; everything else fades.
Double-click a table, or click its name, to open it in the [data browser](/docs/data-browser).

## Large databases

- **Find a table** centres the diagram on it.
- **Neighbours** shows one table and the tables 1, 2 or 3 relations away. A database with
  more than 150 tables opens this way, on its most connected table.
- On PostgreSQL, the schema menu narrows the diagram to one schema.

Small databases are laid out left to right, referencing tables before the tables they
reference; larger ones are arranged around their most referenced tables. Drag cards where you
want them: positions are kept in your browser until you press **Re-layout**.

## Export

The download menu saves the diagram as **PNG** or **SVG**, the whole diagram at full size,
for documentation or a design review.

## Permissions and limits

The diagram shows what your own data access can see, like the data browser: tables you
cannot read are not drawn, and neither are relations to them. Up to 5000 tables, 100000
columns and 20000 foreign keys are read; a larger database shows a notice that only part of
it is drawn.

The data comes from `GET /v1/databases/{id}/diagram`.
